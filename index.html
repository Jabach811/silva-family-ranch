From Rosie

Behind every rancher is a story.

Mine started on a family dairy heifer operation, working alongside my father in the Central Valley.

My parents gave me a Holstein heifer when I graduated. I raised her, sold her, and used the money to buy three more. I kept building from there. Being a five-foot-nothing girl in agriculture meant hearing a lot of no, so I worked other jobs to pay the bills and kept going.

When the dairies around us started closing in 2013, we sold our springers and put the dream on hold. We fed other people's cattle in trade for stock until we had a herd of our own. Today those cattle graze hillsides in Alameda and Contra Costa Counties and irrigated pastures in San Joaquin County.

Now I'm raising beef with my own family and selling it directly to families like yours. What more could I ask for?

RosieRancher, mom, farmer's wife

What we sell

Ranch Direct Beef

Buy a whole, half or quarter of one animal. It goes to a state-certified local butcher, hangs to dry age for 14 to 28 days, then gets cut the way you want it, vacuum sealed and frozen. You pick it up at the butcher shop.

Quarter

The usual starting point. Fits in the freezer compartment of a large fridge plus a little overflow, or comfortably in a small chest freezer.

Ordering beef

How your order works.

Nothing is owed until your share is confirmed. Buy a half or a quarter and we match you with other families.

You send a request

Tell us the size share you want and whether you'd like it grass or grain finished. Takes about two minutes, and nothing is owed yet.

We confirm, then harvest

We check the harvest schedule and reserve your share. Buying a half or a quarter? We match you with other families so it adds up to a whole animal — you never have to find a partner yourself.

Dry age, cut, collect

The carcass hangs 14 to 28 days, then you fill out a cutting sheet and pick your beef up from the butcher shop, vacuum sealed and frozen.
